A TRANSPARENT FORMULA

See how the retainer is built

Reserved hours × hourly rate−Your chosen discount
  1. Value at full rate = reserved hours per month × hourly rate.
  2. Retainer discount = value at full rate × discount percentage. It is zero unless you enter one.
  3. Monthly retainer = value at full rate − retainer discount.
  4. Effective hourly rate = monthly retainer ÷ reserved hours. It equals your rate reduced by the discount.
  5. Effective rate at expected usage (optional) = monthly retainer ÷ the hours you expect to actually work. It appears only if you enter those hours, and it is not calculable at 0 hours.
  6. Break-even actual hours (shown only when you choose a discount) = reserved hours × (100 − discount percentage) ÷ 100. At this usage, your effective rate equals your baseline hourly rate. Below it, your effective rate is higher; above it, lower. A $0 baseline rate has no calculable threshold.
  7. Contract value = monthly retainer × contract length in months.

Worked example: a 20-hour monthly retainer

Suppose you reserve 20 hours each month at $60 per hour and choose a 10% discount. You expect to work 15 hours per month and agree to a six-month term.

  • Value at the full rate: 20 × $60 = $1,200 per month.
  • Chosen discount: $1,200 × 10% = $120.
  • Monthly retainer: $1,200 − $120 = $1,080.
  • Rate if all reserved hours are used: $1,080 ÷ 20 = $54 per hour.
  • Rate at 15 expected hours: $1,080 ÷ 15 = $72 per hour. This is higher because the same retainer covers fewer hours.
  • Break-even usage: 20 × (100 − 10) ÷ 100 = 18 hours. Above 18 hours, the effective rate falls below the $60 baseline; below it, the rate is higher.
  • Six-month contract value: $1,080 × 6 = $6,480, assuming the same monthly terms throughout.

This example is illustrative, not a recommended discount or market price. It excludes taxes, payment fees, unused-hour rollover and any charge for overage. Agree those terms with the client before signing.

This tool does not estimate taxes, market rates or demand, and it cannot tell whether a client will accept the price. It does not price overage or rollover: if your expected hours exceed the reserved hours, the extra-hour rate shown assumes no additional charge, so agree extra-hour billing in advance.

BEFORE YOU SEND IT

Terms to settle early

  • What the hours cover: describe the work, so reserved time does not quietly turn into unlimited scope.
  • Unused hours: decide whether they expire at month end, roll over for a limited time, or are billed anyway.
  • Extra work: state how requests beyond the reserved hours are priced and approved.
  • Review and exit: agree when the price is reviewed and how much notice either side gives to end it.

This is general planning guidance, not legal advice. Have important contracts reviewed where you work.

CHOOSE THE RIGHT PRICING MODEL

Is an hours-based retainer the right fit?

This calculator prices one specific arrangement: a monthly block of reserved hours. It does not calculate a deliverables-only retainer or an access/availability fee; those need their own clearly defined scope and pricing method.

  • Choose an hours-based retainer when the client has ongoing, repeatable work and you can estimate and cap the monthly hours. Agree which tasks count against the hours, what availability means, and how unused or extra hours are handled.
  • Choose a fixed project price when the client wants a defined result with clear deliverables and a natural end. Use the project quote calculator and project pricing guide to scope and estimate it.
  • Choose hourly billing or paid discovery when the scope is still changing, the work is exploratory, or monthly demand is too uncertain to reserve responsibly. Start with the hourly rate calculator to set a baseline, then revisit a retainer once the pattern is clearer.

A monthly invoice alone does not make an arrangement a good retainer. The value comes from a clear recurring need and a limit both sides understand. If the hours vary widely or cannot be capped, gather evidence from actual work before promising a fixed monthly block.

Common questions

Do I have to offer a discount on a retainer?

No. A discount is optional. If you choose one, enter the percentage yourself; the calculator starts at 0% and shows the effective hourly rate so you can see what the discount costs you.

What does the effective hourly rate mean?

It is the monthly retainer divided by the reserved hours. It describes what you earn per reserved hour if every reserved hour is used and the client pays in full, so it is not a guarantee of income.

Why enter the hours I expect to actually work?

Clients rarely use exactly the reserved hours. Entering your expected hours shows what the same monthly fee works out to per hour if you work fewer hours (a higher rate) or more hours (a lower rate). Nothing is assumed for you: the field starts blank and the all-hours rate is always shown too.

What are break-even actual hours?

When you choose a discount, this is the number of hours you can actually work before the effective hourly rate falls below the hourly rate you entered. At the threshold, both rates are equal. Fewer hours means a higher effective rate; more hours means a lower one. It is hidden when there is no discount because the threshold would simply repeat your reserved hours. A $0 hourly rate has no meaningful break-even threshold.

Does the retainer include tax or platform fees?

No automatic tax or fee is applied. Rules and platform fees vary, so confirm the treatment that applies to you.

Are my inputs saved or sent anywhere?

No. Calculations run in your browser. RateNest does not send or store the values you enter.
