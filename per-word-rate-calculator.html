A TRANSPARENT FORMULA

How the hourly rate is worked out

Rate per word × words÷Total hours
  1. Fee for the piece = rate per word × paid word count.
  2. Platform or payment fee = fee for the piece × the percentage you enter. It is zero unless you enter one.
  3. You keep = fee for the piece − platform or payment fee.
  4. Hourly rate = what you keep ÷ total hours for the piece.
  5. Finished words per hour = paid word count ÷ total hours.
  6. Per-word rate for your target (optional) = target hourly rate × total hours ÷ (word count × share you keep).
  7. Words per hour needed (optional) = target hourly rate ÷ (rate per word × share you keep).
  8. Hours that fit your target (optional) = what you keep ÷ target hourly rate.

Worked example: a 1,500-word article

Suppose a client pays $0.10 per word for 1,500 words, and the piece takes 6 hours from first research to final revision. You want to earn $50 per hour.

  • Fee for the piece: 1,500 × $0.10 = $150.
  • Hourly rate: $150 ÷ 6 = $25 per hour.
  • Writing speed: 1,500 ÷ 6 = 250 finished words per hour.
  • Per-word rate for a $50 target: $50 × 6 ÷ 1,500 = $0.20 per word.
  • Speed needed at $0.10 per word: $50 ÷ $0.10 = 500 words per hour.
  • Hours that fit at $150: $150 ÷ $50 = 3 hours.
  • If a platform kept 10%: you keep $135, so the hourly rate is $135 ÷ 6 = $22.50 per hour.

This example is illustrative, not a recommended rate or a market price. It excludes taxes and any unpaid time spent finding the work.

This tool does not estimate taxes, market rates or demand, and it cannot tell whether a client will accept a price. Its result is only as accurate as the hours you enter.

COUNT THE WHOLE JOB

Which hours belong in the total?

Writers often count only the drafting time, which makes a per-word rate look better than it is. Add every hour the piece takes:

  • Research and reading: sources, background and checking facts.
  • Interviews: scheduling, the conversations themselves and transcribing or reviewing notes.
  • Drafting and editing: outline, first draft, your own edits and a final read.
  • Client rounds: revision requests, calls and messages about the piece.
  • Admin you can attribute to the job: sending the invoice, and chasing payment if you want your rate to cover it.

Pitching and finding clients are real work too, but they are hard to attribute to one piece. If you want your rate to cover them, add a share of that time yourself rather than assuming one.

CHOOSE THE RIGHT PRICING MODEL

When does a per-word rate fit?

A per-word rate is simple for the client to compare, but it prices length, not effort. Check the hourly result above before accepting it, especially on research-heavy or interview-heavy work.

  • A per-word rate fits when the piece is predictable: you know the length and the effort per word is fairly steady.
  • A flat project fee fits better when the work is difficult to measure in words, such as interviews, heavy research or several rounds of revision. Use the project quote calculator and the project pricing guide to scope it.
  • A monthly retainer fits when a client needs regular content over time. See the retainer calculator.

Common questions

What is a good per-word rate?

This tool does not know market rates, and rates vary widely by topic, publication, experience and country. A more useful test is your own: does the piece pay at least the hourly rate you need after counting every hour? Enter a target hourly rate to see the per-word rate that would reach it.

The client offers a flat fee instead of a per-word rate. Can I still use this?

Yes. Divide the flat fee by the number of words to get the equivalent per-word rate, enter it here, and add every hour you expect the piece to take. For example, $300 for 1,500 words is $0.20 per word.

Why is there a fee field?

Some platforms and payment services keep a share of what the client pays. If that applies to you, enter the percentage yourself. It starts at 0% because RateNest does not assume any fee rate for you.

Does the result include tax?

No. No tax is calculated or assumed. Rules vary by location, so confirm what applies to you.

Are my inputs saved or sent anywhere?

No. Calculations run in your browser. RateNest does not send or store the values you enter.
